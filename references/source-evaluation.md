## Principle

A source is not evidence merely because it is prestigious, official, peer reviewed, widely cited, or linked. Evaluate fitness for the exact proposition.

## Evaluation dimensions

- **Directness:** Does the source state, show, measure, or document the proposition directly?
- **Authority:** Is it competent and responsible for this specific fact?
- **Primary status:** Is it the original study, dataset, record, legal text, transcript, interview, measurement, or first-party document?
- **Method and inspectability:** Can the reader inspect method, data, sample, definitions, provenance, calculations, and limitations?
- **Currency and version:** Does it cover the relevant time and version? Record publication, update, retrieval, archival, and supersession dates.
- **Completeness and context:** Does it contain qualifications, denominators, comparison groups, populations, exceptions, and surrounding passages?
- **Independence:** Does it have an independent evidence lineage, or repeat the same press release, dataset, interview, or original article?
- **Incentives and conflicts:** Record funding, ownership, advocacy, legal position, author interests, and relevant incentives.
- **Stability and provenance:** Record URL, DOI, identifier, archive, version, access date, and exact passage.

## Source classes

Use these as a starting point, not an automatic ranking:

1. Original records, datasets, transcripts, measurements, legal texts, filings, or original studies.
2. Direct expert or participant evidence with identifiable provenance and corroboration.
3. High-quality secondary reporting or synthesis that names and links evidence.
4. Institutional summaries, press releases, dashboards, and explanatory pages.
5. Anonymous, unattributed, derivative, unsourced, or generated material.

The best source depends on the claim. A press release can establish what an organization announced but does not independently prove the announcement’s claims.

## Support and refutation

For each material claim, create both:

- A support path: what would directly establish the proposition?
- A refutation/context path: what would contradict, narrow, update, or qualify it?

Search exact claims, distinctive phrases, numbers, names, original sources, source domains, dates, archives, and alternative explanations. Search original methods, data, corrections, retractions, and replications for technical claims.

## Citation-support test

1. Does the work exist and match the reference?
2. Is the passage accessible and correctly located?
3. Does it support the sentence as written?
4. Does it support the same population, timeframe, comparison, unit, and certainty?
5. Is a qualification omitted?
6. Is a secondary source presented as primary?
7. Is it corrected, retracted, superseded, or updated?
8. Is the citation being used to create an appearance of support rather than actual support?

## Common failures

Flag citation identity drift, citation existence without support, quote drift, paraphrase inflation, scope lift, unsupported statistics, wrong denominator/unit, stale sources, source repetition masquerading as independence, abstract/headline treated as full-text evidence, correlation described as causation, case study generalized to a population, official statement treated as independent confirmation, expert opinion treated as empirical proof, and search snippets treated as source content.

## Expert use

Experts can clarify methods, definitions, or domain context. Record expertise, conflicts, source access, and disagreement. Expert opinion does not replace primary evidence.
