# Roles, independence, and corrections

## Author or reporter

Responsible for submission accuracy and should provide sources, contacts, recordings/transcripts, source documents, datasets, calculations, notes, and relevant correspondence. Should answer clarification requests and review proposed factual repairs.

## Fact checker

Independently verifies material statements and implications, traces claims to the best available evidence, searches for counterevidence and context, keeps the ledger, identifies failures, reports uncertainty, and escalates premise failure, plagiarism, fabrication, legal risk, and high-stakes issues. Does not silently rewrite style, insert new reporting, strengthen the argument, or decide publication policy alone.

## Editor

Decides whether to accept a repair, request reporting, narrow or remove a claim, delay publication, publish a correction, or escalate. Distinguishes factual correction from interpretive disagreement.

## Expert consultant

Clarifies specialized methods, definitions, or context. Record expertise, conflicts, source access, and disagreement. Expert opinion does not replace primary evidence.

## Claimant, subject, or source

May be offered a reasonable opportunity to provide evidence or correct a factual misunderstanding when safe and appropriate. Record request and response. Denial or nonresponse is not proof.

## Publisher

Owns correction, retraction, update, archive, and public accountability. Corrections should be prompt, visible, specific, dated, and linked to affected material.

## Independence and intent

Use the same standards across people and institutions. Let evidence determine the status. Do not infer deception, fraud, or motive from an inaccurate claim; intent requires separate evidence and scope approval.

## Correction states

Distinguish minor wording correction, material factual correction, new evidence/update, retraction/withdrawal, expression of concern, and correction to the fact-check itself. Preserve an audit trail when practical.
