# Publication-type modules

## Articles, features, news, and opinion

Check headlines, decks, captions, names, roles, dates, places, chronology, quotations, transcription, translation, attribution, numbers, units, denominators, comparisons, links, source support, context, and factual premises embedded in opinion or satire. Do not rate an opinion as false merely because it is debatable.

## Case studies

Check case identity, chronology, participants, setting, intervention or exposure, outcomes, measurements, baselines, missing/excluded cases, consent/privacy, selection effects, rival explanations, causal language, and generalization. A case may be accurate while failing to establish population-wide effectiveness or causation.

## Reports, white papers, policy briefs, and annual reports

Check executive-summary alignment, definitions, scope, timeframe, units, denominators, provenance, collection method, sample, missing data, exclusions, weighting, calculations, tables, charts, projections, correlation/causation, funding, conflicts, limitations, and whether recommendations follow from the evidence. Look for executive summaries that are stronger than the body.

## Journal articles

Check authors, title, journal, volume, issue, pages, DOI, date, version, article type, corrections, retractions, abstract/full-text consistency, hypotheses, protocol/preregistration, design, sample, recruitment, attrition, missing data, measures, operational definitions, analysis, uncertainty, multiplicity, results, supplementary materials, limitations, funding, conflicts, and conclusion scope.

Use APA JARS, CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA, CARE, STARD, TRIPOD, SRQR, COREQ, or other relevant reporting guidance as diagnostic tools. Reporting compliance does not prove truth, validity, absence of bias, or causality.

## Books and chapters

Check edition, printing, revised text, citations, quotations, tables, archival sources, translations, chapter context, and changes between editions. Define scope and sampling before a book-length check.

## Websites and blogs

Check author, ownership, publication/update dates, links, version history, archives, corrections, and whether the page is a primary statement or derivative summary. Record retrieval date.

## Press releases and institutional statements

Treat the release as evidence of what the organization announced. Trace important factual claims to original studies, records, data, or independent reporting. Record commissioning, advocacy, funding, and incentives.

## Datasets and dashboards

Check identity, version, publisher, collection dates, inclusion/exclusion rules, missing data, definitions, units, denominators, transformations, documentation, code, update schedule, revisions, and calculations. A dashboard is not a substitute for underlying data or methodology.

## Legal, regulatory, and standards documents

Check jurisdiction, issuing authority, exact text, effective date, amendments, supersession, definitions, exceptions, and whether a summary is mistaken for controlling text. Do not provide legal advice.

## Interviews and multimedia

Check recording/transcript provenance, identity, date, location, transcription, translation, edits, missing context, direct versus reconstructed quote, original media, date, location, edits, caption, and whether the media establishes the claimed fact.
