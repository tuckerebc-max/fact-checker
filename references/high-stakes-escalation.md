# High-stakes escalation

## Elevated threshold

Use higher evidence and corroboration thresholds for medical, legal, financial, safety, engineering, environmental, election, conflict, criminal, reputational, scientific, child, patient, or vulnerable-person claims.

Do not give advice in these domains. State evidence and limitations, then recommend qualified human review when appropriate.

## Escalate when

- Serious harm could result if the claim is wrong.
- Primary evidence is unavailable or materially conflicting.
- Specialized interpretation is required.
- A source is retracted, corrected, disputed, or suspected of fabrication.
- There is a major premise failure, plagiarism, or possible data manipulation.
- The claim concerns an identifiable private or vulnerable person.
- Publication could create legal exposure.
- A decision depends on values or policy rather than factual verification.

Record trigger, evidence, uncertainty, reviewer, advice, decision owner, and unresolved risk. Do not represent escalation as completed verification.

Minimize personal information in external queries. Use placeholders or generalized terms when identity is not necessary.
